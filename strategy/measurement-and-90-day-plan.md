# Measurement Plan and 90-Day Build Sequence

## Measurement: the five numbers reviewed weekly
1. **Revenue per recipient, per flow.** Not open rate. A flow lives or dies on this.
2. **Second-order rate by day 60.** The single best health check on whether post-purchase is doing its job.
3. **Subscription share of orders, and skip-to-cancel ratio.** Rising skips with flat cancels is good news: the save system is working.
4. **Refund and support-ticket rate on first orders.** Post-purchase's report card.
5. **List engagement quality:** share of the list that opened or clicked in 60 days, and sunset compliance. Deliverability is an asset with a balance.

Every flow ships with a holdout where volume allows: 10% of eligible profiles held out for the first 60 days, so the program can state its incremental value instead of its attributed value. Attribution is a reporting view, not proof.

## The 90 days
**Days 1 to 30, foundation.** Quiz live with the segment map. Welcome and post-purchase built first, because they touch every new customer. Data check: events, quiz properties, and subscription events verified in Klaviyo before any copy is judged.

**Days 31 to 60, recovery.** Browse and cart abandon, second-order push, subscription nurture with the day-30 usage check. First holdout reads. Kill or fix any flow under its revenue-per-recipient floor.

**Days 61 to 90, save and clean.** Save and reactivation flow, winback with real sunset, first campaign calendar built around the routine content (education first, promotion second). Report to leadership on incremental terms: holdout results, second-order rate movement, skip-to-cancel ratio.

## What I would not do in 90 days
No discount ladder, no SMS broadcast program, no loyalty points layer. Each of those spends margin or attention that a young program has not earned yet, and each is much harder to remove than to add later.
