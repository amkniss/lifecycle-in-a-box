# Subscription Save and Reactivation, Email 1 of 3: The Interval Fix Before the Discount
**Trigger:** Subscription cancelled, paused indefinitely, or two consecutive skips. **Send:** 24 hours after the event. **Job:** most "cancellations" in this model are inventory problems. Fix the inventory problem first.

**Subject:** Pause the pile-up, keep the price
**Preheader:** Move to 60 or 90 days and keep your subscriber price locked.

**Body:**
You cancelled your Solmere subscription yesterday, and the most common reason is sitting in your bathroom cabinet: too much product, arriving faster than you use it.

Before we say goodbye, the option most people actually wanted:

**Keep your $54.40 subscriber price, move to every 60 or 90 days.** Your price stays locked at today's rate. Nothing ships until your current bottle is nearly done. One tap, no new checkout:

**CTA:** Switch me to 90 days and keep my price

Not the issue? The other honest options: pause for 60 days with the same price lock, or reply and tell us what it was. Price, irritation, results: each answer fixes a different thing, and we would rather know.

**CTA 2:** Pause 60 days instead

*Flow note: a discount is deliberately not offered in this email. It appears in email 3, and only for the segment whose cancel reason was price. Offering money first trains subscribers to cancel as a negotiation, and it spends margin on people whose problem was a calendar.*
